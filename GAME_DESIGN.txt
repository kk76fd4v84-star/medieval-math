NOVEMBER 5, 2026 — CORE DESIGN RULE — POWER, DIFFICULTY AND RISK
NON-NEGOTIABLE DESIGN PILLAR. Explicitly confirmed by Magnus.
This rule takes precedence over conflicting older balance or handoff notes below.
Do not flatten or remove it during balancing, new modes, upgrades or future ports.

STRONGER ATTACKS REQUIRE HARDER CHALLENGES; DANGEROUS ACTIONS ADD FAILURE RISK.
- Weak swing: easier challenge, lower damage, the safer choice.
- Hard swing: harder challenge, higher damage. The extra power must be earned.
- Powerful spells: hard challenges and dangerous failure consequences.
- Preserve this relationship in maths, word typing and any future challenge mode.
  A change of input format must not turn the strongest attack into a free choice.
  Timing, rhythm and number-sequence alternatives are still discussion ideas;
  this rule does not establish them as implemented or scheduled features.

TEMPORARY HELP AND LASTING PROGRESSION
- Stronger attacks start harder, but temporary buffs and upgrades can make their
  challenges easier to perform. That improvement is intentional player progress.
- Do not silently increase challenge difficulty again to cancel a buff or upgrade.
  Make the distinction between base difficulty and modified difficulty explicit.
- Respect the existing scope of each buff: Scroll of Stupidity affects the next
  maths problem only; mead affects one battle. This rule does not extend either.
- A newly found, very powerful sword or mage staff may be usable immediately but
  demand a formidable challenge. The player can try it and experience that risk.
- A blacksmith can refine that weapon into something more manageable and reliable
  for progression into new biomes. The purpose is to make its powerful action
  practical to use, not merely raise damage or downgrade it into weak ordinary loot.
- Preserve meaningful weak/hard/spell choices as the player progresses. Exact
  damage, difficulty steps, upgrade costs and crafting details remain to be tuned;
  none are invented or confirmed by this note.

FIRE BOTTLES AND PARTY REACTIONS
- Timmy the Firestarter keeps his word-typing fire attacks and bottles/torch role.
  Failed bottle throws can accidentally set the player's own party on fire.
  This is a real gameplay consequence, not only a harmless visual gag.
- Another living companion comments on a teammate's failure with a short, absurd
  in-character written reaction. Keep one speech bubble per speaker, clear of
  faces. Voice acting remains removed. If nobody else is alive, omit the reaction.
- Backfire damage, duration, mitigation and interaction with existing enemy turns
  need explicit balancing; do not guess a numeric rule or stack extra punishments
  accidentally. Keep the current Dark Wizard backfire rules unless separately changed.

READABILITY
Show what an action does, its normal damage, challenge difficulty and meaningful
backfire risk before commitment. Show when a buff or upgrade reduces difficulty.
Use the existing concise UI; do not bury this information in extra menu screens.

IMPLEMENTATION STATUS — DESIGN RECORDED, NOT A GAMEPLAY PATCH
Existing weak/hard challenge differences, buffs and Dark Wizard backfires are the
starting point. Party-fire bottle failures, companion failure reactions, per-weapon
challenge requirements and blacksmith refinement are approved design work to
implement and verify. This documentation update does not claim they are playable.
Do not describe these features as delivered until checked in the actual game.

Future changes must demonstrate: weak is easier than hard at comparable conditions;
high-power actions carry meaningful difficulty/risk; an advertised buff/refinement
actually makes execution easier; failure consequences match the displayed promise.

A HARD DAY'S MATH — CURRENT GAME RULES
Updated 4 November 2026. This document and the current section of HANDOFF.md
supersede older conflicting prototype notes. Values marked provisional are
implementation choices for playtesting, not additional approved design.

ART AND PRESENTATION
Use the approved worn medieval woodcut art: irregular black ink, pale parchment,
sparse dirty orange, carved hatching. Preserve the previous bucket-helmet hero
and its baked walk. The custom humanoid rig experiment was cancelled.
Small eye shadows with a tiny glint; avoid large staring eyes and shiny surfaces.
World above; maths below; the ground and ornamental divider separate them.
Keep small icons simple enough to read at their actual display size.
Maintain solid interiors, matching parchment tint, complete scenery silhouettes
and tapered overlapping ground. No abrupt cut edges or transparent tree bodies.
Near layers move left faster than distant layers when walking right.
The opening is castle/countryside, with long meadow stretches and occasional
village, camp and trees. Canyons belong later; do not rush through biomes.
Background birds are now half their previous display size.

PARTY, HEALTH AND CASUALTIES
Maximum four living members including the player.
The player, ordinary knights and Timmy/firestarters each contribute three hearts.
Priests and executioners wear cloth and contribute two hearts each.
Health is shared. Wounds carry between encounters. Unhealed red damage kills
a companion at their heart threshold. Healing subtracts the health actually
restored from the outstanding damage. It can prevent a pending death, not undo one.
Companions die before the player. Current tie-breaking is the rear companion
first (provisional); the player always dies last.
Victory does not restore health. Recover through priests, stew and camps.
Recruiting and losing members change maximum health by that member's role.
Older saves migrate role health without wiping journey progress or equipment.

TURN-BASED COMBAT
Choose a move, complete its challenge, resolve its effect, then surviving
enemies take their turn. A dead enemy does not retaliate.
A failed sum loses the action and moves the King's hunters one block closer;
then the ordinary enemy turn happens, not an additional punishment attack.
The hunter tracker is a dotted route between the hunters and the party.
Keep a brutal roguelite feel: few lifelines, meaningful losses, early failure
when the player cannot solve the challenges.
Moves stay on the same screen in at most two rows of four. The prompt is
smaller and higher. Hide class abilities unless the required members are alive.
A temporarily unavailable unlocked move can be disabled (full-health Heal,
Resurrect with no casualty). Do not present a separate difficulty menu.

ORDINARY KNIGHTS
Weak swing: easier maths, weaker damage. Hard swing: harder maths, more damage.
The hero counts as an ordinary knight for matching bonuses.
Each additional living matching attacker adds 50% of that class's base damage:
one = 100%, two = 150%, three = 200%, four = 250%, not compounded multipliers.
Matching ordinary knights strengthen both sword swings. They do not strengthen
Timmy's fire or an executioner's axe. Matching firestarters/executioners apply
the same additive rule to their own attacks. Equipment still affects power.
Current provisional sword values: base weak 3 + floor(first knight base/4) + mean knight weapon level,
hard = weak base x 2 + 1, then the matching-knight multiplier. Sword hits the
front two living foes when a fight has several enemies.

FRANK THE MAD PRIEST
Find and recruit Frank as a companion before the optional dragon encounter.
No priest: no Heal and no Sacrifice, including keyboard shortcuts.
One priest: Heal and priest-only Sacrifice.
Two living priests: additionally unlock Resurrect, with harder maths.
Three living priests: priest maths becomes easier.
Priests do NOT receive a +50% healing-amount bonus. Their progression is utility:
healing, resurrection, then easier questions.
Current provisional Heal: up to two hearts, two uses per battle; random
easy/normal/hard question with one or two priests, easy with three.
Current provisional Resurrect: once per battle, restores the most recent
eligible casualty from that battle with ONE heart, provided the party has room.
Two priests use hard maths; three use normal maths. A revived three-heart knight
is wounded: one further unhealed damage can kill him. Healing can prevent it.
Resurrect takes the turn, so surviving enemies act afterwards.
Sacrifice lets a priest take one for the team; ordinary knights and Timmy
must never be offered as sacrifices. Losing the last priest removes Heal.
Additional priest recruits make the two- and three-priest tiers reachable.
Frank has a full-height lean build and an orange hood worn up.

TIMMY THE FIRESTARTER
Use the chainmail-coif character, carrying bottles and a torch.
His attacks use WORD TYPING, not arithmetic. The earlier "two words" reading
was incorrect. There is no arbitrary two-use limit.
Current provisional challenge: one word, seven seconds, fire damage to every
living foe on completion. A failed word fizzles, then enemies take their turn.
The existing fire failure has no additional hunter step (provisional).
Fire damage starts at 3 plus the strongest living firestarter weapon level,
then the matching-firestarter bonus. No purchasable fire bottle is required.
Legacy bottle inventory may remain in old saves but cannot unlock this move.

THE EXECUTIONER
Black cloth hood and clothes; two hearts; axe attack.
Only multiplication problems, including while a Stupidity effect is active.
Execute is greyed out above 50% health and available at 50% or less. It targets
the front living enemy, deals double damage, and uses multiplication. Guard
both the button/shortcut and answer resolution against an ineligible target.
Current provisional base: 3 plus strongest executioner weapon level, matching
executioner bonus, then the wounded-target multiplier. Hits one front enemy.

RIDDLE ENCOUNTERS
Only the approved black-robed, one-eyed keeper behind his separate rock asks
riddles. He occupies all three existing riddle stops, including the former
enchanter and hermit spots. Keep their positions, rewards and question pool.

RECRUITMENT AND REST STOPS
Talk to a candidate, ask them to join, solve their maths. If that fails, offer
the D20 contest. Recruitment respects the four-member limit. Only one successful
recruit per tavern stop per run: maths and dice both use that choice. Failures
do not use it. Leaving/re-entering and saving/loading do not reset it. Other
taverns have their own choice; camp/shop/road recruitment is unchanged.
